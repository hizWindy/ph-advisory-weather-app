Top 3 findings

1. Rain probability and timing are the most important day-to-day weather information
   
Evidence: 27 of 31 respondents (87%) mentioned rain, chance of rain, rainfall, or when rain would occur when asked which weather information mattered most. Temperature or heat was also mentioned by 13 of 31 respondents (42%).

What respondents need: They want to know whether it will rain, approximately when it will start, and how hot it will be so they can decide whether to bring an umbrella or raincoat, what to wear, and whether to adjust travel or daily plans.

Product implication: Make the rain forecast prominent. Show chance of rain and expected timing/intensity by hour, alongside current temperature and heat information.

2. People need timely alerts because sudden rain and severe weather disrupt travel and daily activities
   
Evidence: 26 of 31 respondents (84%) explicitly described experiencing sudden rain, heavy rain, or related disruption. Separately, 20 of 31 respondents (65%) requested real-time updates, early warnings, time-specific notifications, reminders, or similar alert features.

What respondents need: Alerts that arrive before conditions worsen—not only a general daily forecast. Respondents also mentioned storm and flood warnings, and information that helps them decide whether to travel, bring protection, or avoid risky areas.

Product implication: Prioritize configurable push notifications for approaching rain and severe-weather conditions. Where forecast data supports it, include expected timing, intensity, affected area, and practical advice. Avoid implying certainty when the forecast is probabilistic.

3. Weather information must be clear, reliable, and relevant to the user's location
   
Evidence: 16 of 31 respondents (52%) described a specific information gap involving unclear, inaccurate, delayed, incomplete, or insufficient local weather information.

What respondents need: Simple wording instead of unexplained technical terms, information that is easy to scan, reliable updates, and alerts that reflect conditions in the user's area. Some respondents also raised access concerns when internet connectivity is limited.

Product implication: Use plain Filipino/English labels and brief explanations for terms such as humidity and heat index. Show the location covered, source of the advisory, and the time it was last updated. Explore lightweight/offline access to saved emergency guidance where feasible.

Recommended priorities

Rain-first forecast: Clearly display chance of rain, hourly timing, and expected intensity, with temperature/heat information nearby.
Actionable alerts: Send timely, location-relevant notifications for rain, storms, and flood risks, with a practical next step where appropriate.
Trust and comprehension: Use clear language, visible update timestamps and information sources, and local-level detail where reliable data is available.
